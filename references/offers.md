# Offers and Pain-Point Mining

Two jobs: (1) find the real pain in their words, (2) turn sender's service into an outcome offer they can't easily ignore.

## Contents
1. Pain-point mining prompts
2. Output -> outcome: offer formula
3. Outcome offers by niche
4. Value-first assets (lead magnets for cold email)
5. Honesty rules for offers

---

## 1. Pain-point mining prompts

Run these on research notes (reviews, site, socials, job posts, competitor sites) before writing. Answer each internally; use the answers in the email.

1. "List every complaint, frustration, or friction in this business's public reviews and comments. Quote the exact words customers use."
2. "Acting as the owner of [business] in [town], what keeps you up at night this month? Consider season, staffing, competitors, cash flow."
3. "What does a lost customer cost this business? Estimate avg ticket, repeat visits, lifetime value. Label assumptions."
4. "Where does a customer drop off between 'searches Google' and 'pays'? Walk every step: find -> understand -> trust -> contact/book -> show up -> return/review."
5. "What does the top competitor do that this business doesn't, that a website or automation could fix?"
6. "Rewrite the top pain in the owner's own words, as they'd say it to a friend." (Use this phrasing in the email.)
7. SaaS: "From G2/Reddit/job posts, what is this team's biggest growth bottleneck: traffic, conversion, activation, retention, or team bandwidth?"

Pick the pain that scores highest on: felt daily + costs money + sender can fix.

## 2. Output -> outcome: offer formula

Owners don't buy "a website" or "automation". They buy more customers, less admin, beating the competitor.

| Output (weak) | Outcome (strong) |
|---|---|
| "We build websites" | "Turn Google searches in [town] into booked jobs" |
| "We do SEO" | "Show up in the top 3 when people search '[service] [town]'" |
| "We set up automation" | "Every missed call gets a text-back in 10 seconds, so the job doesn't go to the next guy" |
| "We design landing pages" | "More trial signups from the traffic you already have" |
| "We build chatbots" | "Answer pricing and booking questions at 11pm without you" |

**Offer formula**
`[Specific outcome] for [specific niche/town] in [timeframe], [risk reducer].`

Examples:
- "10 more booked quote requests in 30 days for lawn crews in Frisco, or I keep working free until you do." (only if sender actually offers this guarantee)
- "Pickup ordering live for Bean There in 7 days - you only pay once it's live."
- "A comparison page that ranks for 'Asana alternative for agencies', built in 2 weeks."

**Risk reducers** (use only what sender actually offers): free mockup first, pay after launch, monthly no lock-in, performance guarantee, free audit, first month free.

If sender hasn't told you their guarantee/pricing terms, write the outcome without a guarantee and add a `[risk reducer?]` note for the user.

## 3. Outcome offers by niche

| Niche | Outcome angle |
|---|---|
| SaaS | more trial signups / demo bookings from existing traffic; rank for competitor-alternative searches; onboarding that activates trials; save X hrs/week of manual ops |
| Cafe | more pickup pre-orders, regulars coming back on quiet weekdays, top-3 on Maps |
| Restaurant / cloud kitchen | direct orders without aggregator commission, table bookings online |
| Bakery | custom orders with deposit, no DM back-and-forth |
| Lawn / landscaping | more quote requests, missed calls rescued, spring slots filled early, more Google reviews |
| Plumber / HVAC / electrician | after-hours emergencies captured, maintenance-plan reminders filling slow months |
| Cleaning | instant quotes -> more bookings, recurring clients auto-scheduled |
| Salon / barber / spa | fewer no-shows, rebooking on autopilot, weekday slots filled |
| Gym / studio | trial leads contacted in 5 min, lapsed members won back |
| Dental / clinic | online booking, recall reminders, front desk freed up |
| Real estate | instant lead response, own-brand site vs portals |

## 4. Value-first assets (lead with value, not a pitch)

Default first email offers one of these instead of asking for a call. Ask permission to send ("Want it?") rather than attaching or linking cold.

- **Mini audit**: 3 specific fixes for their site/Google profile (can be in the email body itself)
- **Mockup**: homepage, booking page, or menu page redesigned (screenshot)
- **Loom video**: 2-min walkthrough of their site with fixes
- **Competitor snapshot**: "what [competitor] does that you don't" 3-bullet list
- **Missed-revenue estimate**: one-page rough math (labelled estimate)
- **Rewritten headline/copy**: their hero section rewritten
- **Review summary**: what their reviews say customers love/hate
- **Template/resource**: e.g. "spring cleanup email you can send to past customers"

The call ask comes AFTER they accept the asset (reply #1 or follow-up), not in email 1. Exception: a warm trigger event with high fit can ask for a short call directly.

Make sure the promised asset can actually be produced - list it in "Before sending" so user prepares it.

---

## 5. Honesty rules for offers

- Never promise numbers the sender can't back up. "10 appointments in 30 days" only if sender has delivered similar and agrees to the claim
- Guarantees only if sender confirmed them
- "Free" only if truly free, no catch
- Estimates labelled ("rough math", "even 2 jobs a week")
- If unsure, use outcome language without numbers: "more quote requests from people already searching for you"
